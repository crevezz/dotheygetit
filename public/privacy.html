Privacy notice

Plain English. Last updated 27 October 2026.

The short version. A pupil types their name and their answers. The answers are sent to an AI service to be read, and the result is shown to the teacher who set the check. The app itself does not sell or share anything. Pupil answer pages load no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts. The teacher side uses Google Analytics, but only after a teacher accepts cookies, and never on a pupil page.

Our public website at dotheygetit.app is different. It uses Google Analytics and the Meta pixel, but only if you accept cookies. The next section explains exactly what they do.

The public website: analytics and advertising

Google Analytics also runs on the teacher side of the app at app.dotheygetit.app, under the same rule: nothing loads until a teacher clicks Accept, and it never loads on a pupil answer page or the pupil tab. The Meta pixel does not run in the app at all.

Neither tool is loaded until you click Accept on the cookie banner. If you click Reject, neither script is loaded at all. We use Google Consent Mode v2, which tells Google that analytics and advertising storage are denied until you make a choice.

Google Analytics (GA4)

  • What it is for: understanding how the public website is used - which pages are read, how people arrive, and what is working - so we can improve it.
  • What it collects: the pages you visit and how long you spend on them, the site or search that referred you, your approximate location derived from your IP address, your device, browser and screen size, and a random client ID kept in first-party cookies (_ga and _ga_...). We do not send pupil names, answers or any other pupil data to Google Analytics.
  • Who processes it: Google Ireland Limited and Google LLC. Data may be transferred outside the UK under the UK International Data Transfer Addendum or UK-approved standard contractual clauses.
  • How long it is kept: Google Analytics event data is retained for up to 14 months, depending on the retention setting on our property, and is then deleted.

Meta pixel

  • What it is for: measuring how well our advertising works - for example, whether someone who saw an advert later visited the website or signed up.
  • What it collects: the pages you visit, your device and browser information, your IP address, and identifiers stored in first-party cookies (_fbp and _fbc). Meta may connect this with your Meta account if you have one.
  • Who processes it: Meta Platforms Ireland Limited and Meta Platforms, Inc. Data may be transferred outside the UK under UK-approved safeguards such as the International Data Transfer Addendum or standard contractual clauses.
  • How long it is kept: Meta retains event data in line with its own retention schedule. See Meta's privacy policy for details.

Changing your choice

You can accept or reject at any time using the Cookie settings link in the footer of the public website. Rejecting does not stop you using the site. You can also clear cookies and site data in your browser, which removes the saved choice; you will be asked again on your next visit.

What is collected

  • The pupil's name - whatever they type when they join. It does not have to be a full name; a first name, or a first name and initial, is enough for a teacher to know who is who.
  • Their answers to the teacher's questions, and the questions themselves.
  • What the AI made of the answers - the reading the teacher sees on the results card.

Nothing else. No photographs, no dates of birth, no addresses, no school records. Pupils do not have accounts and do not log in.

What happens to it

  • It is sent to an AI service. The questions, the answers and the pupil's name are sent to a third-party model provider (through OpenRouter) so the answers can be read and marked. That is a transfer of the answers to another company, and it is the whole point of the tool - without it there is no marking.
  • It is stored on the server that runs this site, in a single data file, so the teacher can open the results. It is not encrypted at rest.
  • The teacher who set the check can see it, and can change the colour a pupil was given.

Who can see it

The teacher who set the check, and the AI provider named above. Nobody else. There is no public page, no search, no directory and no sharing link.

How long it is kept

  • It stays until it is deleted. A teacher can delete a check or a whole class from the results screen, and that removes the answers with it.
  • It is stored in a single data file on the server that runs the app. One backup copy is made each day and the last 14 daily copies are kept; older copies are automatically deleted. The data file is not encrypted at rest. If the hosting service is restarted or redeployed without persistent storage, recent data can be lost - so do not treat this as a permanent record. Export anything you need to keep.
  • Teacher accounts are separate: an email and a password, stored hashed, and a sign-in cookie that keeps a teacher logged in for 30 days. The cookie is not used to track anyone.

What is not done

  • No advertising and no adverts of any kind inside the app or on pupil answer pages.
  • No analytics on pupil answer pages, and no advertising anywhere in the app. Google Analytics runs on the teacher side only, and only if the teacher accepts cookies. The Meta pixel is on the public website only.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of data with anyone beyond the AI provider above.
  • No profiling of pupils, and no decisions made about a pupil by a computer alone.

If you are the teacher

  • Only put in what you need. A first name is enough.
  • The wording the pupils see is: their answers go to their teacher and to an AI that helps read them.
  • The colour is guidance, not a judgement. The AI can be wrong in both directions, and you can change any colour by hand.
  • Your school's own policy comes first. If it does not allow pupil answers to be sent to an external AI service, do not use this tool with pupil data.

If you are a pupil or a parent

Your teacher set this check. What you type is seen by your teacher, and by the AI that helps them read it. If you would rather not take part, say so to your teacher - you do not have to use this.

Mailing list

On the public website at dotheygetit.app you can ask for a GCSE misconception bank as a PDF, the New Teacher Survival Pack as a PDF, or a short weekly classroom check. If you do, the form collects:

  • Your first name and email address.
  • For the weekly check, your school and the subject you teach, if you choose to give them. Both are optional on that form.
  • For a misconception bank, the subject you choose. That choice is required, so the right PDF can be offered. School is not asked on that form.
  • That you ticked the box agreeing to occasional emails, and the time the form was sent.
  • On the misconception-bank and survival-pack forms, whether you ticked the optional box “Send me occasional teaching tips from Get It? (unsubscribe anytime)”. That box starts unticked. If you tick it, the time you did is stored as well.

The PDF email is sent either way. Teaching tips are sent only if you ticked that optional box: one on day 2, day 5, day 9 and day 14, then one every 14 days. Leave it unticked and you get the PDF and nothing further from that list. Each tip has an unsubscribe link that stops them straight away. You can also write to support@dotheygetit.app.

That is used to send the PDF you asked for and the emails you asked for. It is not sold, and it is not used to advertise to anyone else. It is kept apart from anything a pupil types into a check. The form is stored by our website host, Netlify, Inc., and submissions may be processed outside the UK under Netlify's own safeguards. The tip list is stored there too. The lawful basis is your consent. You can unsubscribe from any email, or by writing to support@dotheygetit.app, and the address is deleted.

Getting something removed

Ask the teacher who set the check. They can delete it from the results screen.

Questions about any of this, or want something removed? Email support@dotheygetit.app.
