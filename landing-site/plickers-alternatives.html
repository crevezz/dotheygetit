Plickers alternatives: a fair look at free checks that are not cards

Plickers became popular for a good reason. Every pupil answers, nobody needs a tablet, and you can see the spread without taking books in. A good alternative depends on the constraint: boards or cards if pupils have no device, a quiz for recall, or a short written check if you need the reason rather than A to D.

SW Sam Whitfield8 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

People searching for a Plickers alternative usually want one of those three things. They are different products. This page separates them.

What Plickers actually is

Each pupil has a paper card with a code, and A, B, C or D around the edges. They rotate it so their answer is at the top. You scan the room with the Plickers app on the teacher's device. The code's orientation is the answer, and the card number is the pupil. Nobody in the class needs an account or a device. Plickers says a session can run offline.

On their help pages, checked on 16 November 2026, the core stays free for teachers: cards, questions, scanning, results. A free set holds up to five questions, and you can make as many as you like. There are 63 card designs, so a larger class needs a workaround. Pro lifts the five-question cap on a set. The price is on their site, in dollars.

It is a fair way to run a planned multiple-choice hinge when pupil devices are a bad idea and you can live with a letter rather than a sentence. Cards can run that hinge. They are a poor way to hear the reason.

Where the cards run out

A letter is not a reason. One in four will be right by luck, more if your distractors are weak. Two pupils can hold opposite models and land on the same letter if the options were written carelessly. Scanning is slower in a restless room than a demo suggests, and the answer is still held up for the row behind. Cards go missing, and you have to write the questions beforehand. If you need no pupil devices, a letter from everyone, and options you will write, Plickers is a fair answer.

Mini whiteboards, when you need no pupil devices

If the constraint is no pupil devices, try boards before another website. Everyone answers, you see working rather than only a letter, and there is nothing to print except, once, the boards themselves. They are public, so copying is easy, and the answer disappears when they wipe. For a method or a diagram they beat cards. For a private confession of confusion they do not.

A quiz game is a different job

Kahoot is what people open when they want energy. Pupils join a live game with a PIN and do not need an account to play. Faster correct answers score more, so the careful pupil loses to the fast one. Kahoot's schools pricing page, checked on 16 November 2026, lists a free plan, Kahoot! Go, with a live cap of 40 participants, and quiz or true/false questions. Paid plans raise the cap. Those numbers move, so check the page. Quizizz does the same kind of job: energy and recall, with pupil devices and usually a score. Use either for public recall, not as the hinge of a fragile idea. You will not have their sentence.

Other tools people compare

Socrative is a quick formative quiz, often multiple choice or short text, once every pupil has a device. Mentimeter is a live poll or word cloud, built for a presentation. Useful as a show of hands at scale, not as a planned hinge, and not a reason in a pupil's own words. Check those sites for current limits.

Forms you may already have

If the school is on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, Forms is already included. Short-text questions are allowed, so you are not stuck with A to D. Pupils often sign in with a school account, which gives you a real name and the "I can't log in" delay that cards were invented to avoid. Responses collect in a sheet, and you still have to read them. Nothing in Forms tells you, in one glance, who needs you before the bell.

A check that asks for the sentence

If you wanted everyone to commit, and you are tired of the letter, ask for the explanation. That needs a pupil device and a connection, so it is the wrong swap where there is a ban on pupil devices. Where devices exist, it answers a question cards cannot, and it is one way of checking for understanding.

Get It? is built for that swap, not as a clone of the cards. Pupils join with a code, no account, and answer in their own words. In about two minutes you see, by name, who has got it, who is unsure, and who needs help, in green, amber or red. It is free for a limited time, no card.

A first name and the answer are there for you, and an AI service reads the answers. If school policy does not allow pupil answers to go to an external AI service, do not use it. Use the boards or the cards. The privacy page sets out how answers are handled. For how you know who understood, a letter is only part of the picture.

Which alternative, in one line each

  • Keep Plickers if you need letters, no pupil devices, and you will write the options properly.
  • Mini whiteboards if you need working or a sketch, and can tolerate public answers.
  • A quiz game if you want pace and recall, not a diagnosis.
  • Forms if you need a stored short answer and can bear the logins.
  • A two-minute explanation check if you need the reason, the name, and a chance to reteach before they leave.
Constraint A fair fit
No pupil devices Plickers or mini whiteboards
Energy and recall A quiz game such as Kahoot or Quizizz

Pick the constraint first. The tool is the easy part.

Questions people ask

What is a good alternative to Plickers?

It depends which part you need. For a fast public answer with no pupil devices, mini whiteboards do the job the cards do, and you can see working rather than only A to D. For a private explanation in pupils' own words, a short digital check is a better fit, but every pupil needs a device. For a noisy recall quiz, a game such as Kahoot is built for that, and it is a different job.

Is Plickers free?

Plickers says the core stays free for teachers: print the cards, build questions, scan answers, and see results. On the free plan a set holds up to five questions, and you can make as many sets as you like. A class can use up to 63 cards. A paid Pro plan lifts the five-question cap on a set. Check their site for the current price.

Do pupils need a device for Plickers?

No. That is the point of it. Pupils hold paper cards. The teacher uses their own device to scan. If your school bans pupil devices and you still have your own, Plickers can fit. Tools that need every pupil to open a link will not.

What are the limitations of Plickers?

Pupils answer multiple choice by rotating a card, so you get a letter rather than a reason, and a guess is one in four. You scan with an app on the teacher's device, which takes longer in a restless room than a demo suggests. Cards get lost. A free set stops at five questions. None of that makes it a bad tool. It makes it a bad tool for the wrong question.

When should I keep Plickers instead of switching?

Keep it if you need a letter from everyone, you cannot or will not give pupils a device, and you will write the options properly. An alternative that needs thirty devices is a different lesson, not a like-for-like swap.

How is a written check different from Plickers?

Plickers tells you which letter they chose. A written or spoken check asks them to explain the idea in their own words, so a guess is harder to hide. It needs a device and a connection, and it is the wrong swap in a room with no pupil devices.

Should I use Plickers or mini whiteboards?

Use boards if you need working or a sketch. Use cards if you need a planned A to D from a roster, with no pupil devices.

Is Kahoot a good alternative to Plickers?

Kahoot is for energy and recall, usually with pupil devices and a speed score. Plickers is a quieter multiple-choice scan with no pupil devices. Neither gives you a sentence.

Try it on one class.

One lesson. One question. Two minutes. If it doesn't tell you something you didn't already know, walk away.
