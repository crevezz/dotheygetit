30 pupils x 3 difficulty classes (easy / medium / hard) - findings
==================================================================

Run: node eval/three-tier-30.js     (real AI on every chat turn and every verdict)
30 pupils, mixed ability poor->excellent (2 gave nothing, 6 poor, 6 weak,
6 middling, 5 good, 5 excellent). Short answers. One question per tier:
  easy    "Which is bigger, 1/2 or 1/4? How do you know?"
  medium  "Which is bigger, 3/4 or 4/5? Tell me how you know."
  hard    "Put these in order, smallest to biggest: 3/4, 5/7, 0.8. Explain."

Wall clock 26.9s. 90 gradings. Agreement with the teacher band: 59/90 (66%).

PER TIER (teacher band -> AI verdict)
  easy    red->red 8  green->green 5  green->amber 7  green->red 4  amber->red 4  amber->amber 2
  medium  red->red 10 green->green 10 amber->green 5  red->amber 4  amber->amber 1
  hard    red->red 13 amber->amber 5  green->green 5  amber->red 4  red->amber 1  amber->green 2

  The middle tier is the honest one. Easy is graded MORE harshly than medium.

DOES DIFFICULTY BITE? (2=green, 1=amber, 0=red)
  easy 0.63   medium 1.17   hard 0.67
  Not monotonic - the class scored HIGHER on medium than on easy. The easy
  question is the one it gets wrong.

WHAT IT GETS RIGHT
  - pupils who gave nothing (Amira, Megan) -> red on all three tiers, every time
  - wrong-reasoning pupils stay red/amber; nobody who clearly understood was
    handed a green for nothing
  - the class spreads across all three colours on every tier

WHAT IT GETS WRONG (real, repeatable)

1. The easy mark scheme is generated WRONG, and it fails correct pupils.
   The auto-drafted marks for the easy question were:
     ["1/2 is bigger, The bottom number (denominator) is the same",
      "the bigger top number (numerator) is the bigger fraction"]
   "The denominator is the same" is FALSE for 1/2 vs 1/4 - they are 2 and 4.
   So a pupil who answers correctly, "1/2. A half is bigger than a quarter.",
   hits neither point and is graded RED. This is the whole reason easy scores
   worst. Green pupils failed on easy: Omar, Nia (excellent); Ravi, Jonah (good).
   Fix: sanity-check generated mark points against the actual question before
   they are used to grade anyone.

2. A bare right guess is passed. "4/5" with no reasoning -> amber, for pupils a
   teacher would call red (Jack, Layla, Leon, Bilal on medium). Right number,
   nothing else, is still worth a colour.

3. Fluent method-words are passed as green. "4/5. Convert both fractions to a
   common denominator and compare the numerators." -> green (Sofia, Freya on
   medium) with no working shown. The words carry the mark.

4. Chatter suppresses a correct answer. Prepending "I like maths when it is not
   fractions." dropped Callum from green to amber on easy, and Tyler from amber
   to red. The marker is distracted by words that are not the answer.

5. Flip-flopping collapses a good pupil. "1/2, because quarters are smaller
   pieces. No wait, the other one." -> red (Jonah, easy). A change of mind with
   the right answer in it is thrown away.

FIX FIRST: the easy-tier mark scheme (finding 1). It is the single biggest cause
of wrong grades in this run and it is the same bug the one-topic run already
logged ("spurious points": a point counted for words that do not carry it).


==================================================================
A/B: does upgrading the model help? does a critic agent help?
==================================================================
Same 30 pupils x 3 tiers each time. Agreement = AI verdict matches the teacher band.

  run                          verdict model        critic   agree   green->red  two-band  wall
  baseline (broken easy marks) gemini-2.5-flash     no       59/90   4           4         27s
  model upgrade only           gemini-3.8-flash     no       47/90   1           14        191s
  critic + keep strict verdict gemini-2.5-flash     yes      55/90   0           2         37s

WHAT THE MODEL UPGRADE DID (2.5-flash -> 3.8-flash)
  - Made it WORSE overall (66% -> 52%) and 7x slower (27s -> 191s).
  - It is far too LENIENT: 13 pupils a teacher would call red were passed as green
    (bare guesses like "4/5" and "1/2 I think" came back green).
  - The model was never the problem: for grading short primary answers the task is
    instruction-following and consistency, not world knowledge. A newer model did
    not help and cost 7x the latency. Upgrading the VERDICT model was a mistake.

WHAT THE CRITIC DID (a second agent that audits the mark scheme)
  - FIXED THE REAL BUG. The easy-tier scheme came back as
      ["states that 1/2 is bigger",
       "shows that 1/2 = 2/4, or explains that dividing into fewer parts makes each part bigger"]
    - both true, both answerable. green->red failures went 4 -> 0 and two-band
    misses 4 -> 2. The easy tier's green->green rose from 5 to 8.
  - So the fix is at the RUBRIC, not the grade. One extra generation-time call,
    off the hot path, is worth more than any number of graders voting.

STILL OPEN
  - The hard-tier points are still being split ("...showing 5/7 = 0.71, 3/4 = 0.75,
    and 0.8" came back as three fragments). Fixed in code (protect commas in
    brackets + tell the critic not to use commas) but not yet re-verified.
  - 2 red pupils still passed as green; the grader prompt is still slightly lenient.
  - Difficulty still does not bite monotonically (easy 1.00, medium 1.03, hard 0.83).

VERDICT ON THE ORIGINAL QUESTIONS
  1. The model was old but the model was not the bug - do NOT swap the verdict model
     for a newer one; this task wants strict + fast, and 3.8-flash was neither.
     Keeping the newer CHEAP brain (3.1-flash-lite) for asking questions is fine.
  2. Do NOT build a standing grading swarm - the app already ensembles correctly by
     counting mark points in code. The ONE agent worth adding was a rubric critic,
     and it is the change that actually moved the failures.

==================================================================
RUN 4: critic + tightened grader prompt (original models)
==================================================================
  agreement 56/90 (62%).  two-band 3.  wall 49.6s.

  FIXED: nobody who gave nothing was passed (0 red->green, was 2). The bare-guess
  rule worked - a bare right answer is no longer green.
  FIXED: hard-tier points are clean now (2 points, no fragments).

  REGRESSED: 3 green pupils graded red again (was 0). The stricter rule plus a
  narrow rubric point - easy point 2 demands the exact words about 2/4 - now
  fails a good pupil who justified it a different valid way (halves are bigger
  pieces). The rubric must accept ANY valid justification, not one phrasing.

  CONCLUSION: prompt tweaks are whack-a-mole. Run-to-run spread is about +/-5
  percent, which swamps each individual tweak (66, 61, 62 all overlap). The two
  durable wins are in CODE, not prompts: (1) the rubric critic, (2) the splitter
  fix. The next real lever is rubric SPECIFICITY - points must accept any sound
  route to the idea - enforced in code, not by asking the model nicely.
