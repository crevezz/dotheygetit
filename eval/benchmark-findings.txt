BENCHMARK - first frozen run (eval/benchmark.js)
================================================
100 pupils x 3 tiers = 300 gradings, 102s, real AI on every turn.
Rubric DRAFTED once, frozen to benchmark-rubric.json, reused forever after.
Config: original models (2.5-flash-lite brain, 2.5-flash verdict) + critic (3.8-flash).

  AGREEMENT        211/300  (70.3%)
  too lenient      0     (no red pupil handed green)
  too harsh        2     (green pupils failed)
  two bands out    2
  repeatability    57/60 answers graded the same every time  (95%)
  difficulty       easy 1.07  medium 0.91  hard 0.64   <- now falls monotonically

WHY THIS RUN IS TRUSTWORTHY AND THE EARLIER ONES WERE NOT
  Every earlier run re-drafted the mark points, so no two runs tested the same
  thing. The score bounced 66 / 61 / 62 and the bounce WAS the rubric. Freeze the
  rubric and the number stops bouncing: change the app, re-run, it moves for one
  reason only.

LEARNING 1 - THE GRADER IS NOT NOISY, IT IS SYSTEMATICALLY WRONG IN ONE WAY
  Repeatability is 95%: the same answer text gets the same colour nearly every
  time. So the errors are not randomness, they are a consistent bug - which is
  good news, because a consistent bug can be fixed.
  The dominant error, by a mile, is: A LUCKY GUESS SCORES A POINT.
    "1/2 I think"     -> amber  (hits "1/2 is bigger")
    "4/5"             -> amber  (hits "4/5 is bigger")
    "0.8 is biggest." -> amber  (hits one of three hard points)
  A poor pupil who guesses right is marked amber - "partly there" - for the same
  answer a poor pupil who guesses WRONG gets red for. The app cannot tell a lucky
  guess from partial understanding. That single mechanism is most of the 89 misses.
  THIS IS THE THING TO FIX NEXT: the answer-only point should not, on its own, lift
  a pupil off red. Reaching amber should need the WORKING point, not the answer point.

LEARNING 2 - DIFFICULTY NOW BITES
  easy 1.07 > medium 0.91 > hard 0.64. The earlier "failure" was an artefact of the
  broken easy rubric (it was failing correct pupils), not of the app's difficulty
  handling. With a sound rubric the curve is exactly right.

THE REAL ASSET
  bench-default.json is a frozen failure corpus - the 89 answers the app actually
  gets wrong, with expected vs got and the note. That is a regression suite made of
  reality. Any change can be re-run against it: node eval/benchmark.js 100.

HOW TO A/B A CHANGE
  The rubric is shared, so two configs are compared on identical items:
    set VERDICT_MODEL=google/gemini-2.5-flash && node eval/benchmark.js 100
  Compare bench-<label>.json agreement, falseGreen and falseRed.

==================================================================
CHANGE: green follows the ANSWER; explanation is a separate flag
==================================================================
Before: bare right answer -> amber (green needed answered AND explained).
After : bare right answer -> GREEN, plus a separate flag = did they say why.

  too harsh (correct pupil failed)   0   <- was 2. The goal: no right answer punished.
  repeatability                      100%
  right, no reason                   44 of 300
    by ability:  poor 22, weak 10, ok 7, good 3, excellent 2

THE FLAG WORKS. 32 of the 44 no-reason greens are the poor and weak bands -
the guessers - and only 2 are excellent. So the flag lands on the pupils who got
it right without understanding, and barely touches the ones who understand. That
is the at-risk list, and it is clean.

WHAT MOVED WHERE:
  the COLOUR now means did they get it right (so it no longer falls with
  difficulty - easy 1.22 medium 1.20 hard 1.28 - because right is right).
  the FLAG now carries the understanding signal.
  Agreement vs the old teacher bands reads 66.7% (was 70.3%), but the old bands
  encoded UNDERSTANDING and the colour no longer does - so it is a different
  metric, not a regression. Compare too-harsh (2 -> 0) and the flag breakdown.

==================================================================
CHANGE: comparison questions - a correct pupil was scored 0 (three bugs)
==================================================================
See eval/comparison-fix.txt for the detail. Short version:

  1. "1/2 because 2 is smaller" was read as the pupil saying 1/2 is SMALLER,
     which contradicted the point "1/2 is bigger", which voided the whole
     question. Fixed with subjectOf() - the thing being compared is what comes
     after the last connective, not the first fraction in the phrase.
  2. Skipping a question made the re-pairing step past the NEXT question, so
     pairing failed and the LLM marker switched off for the WHOLE transcript.
     A pupil who skipped Q1 and aced Q2 got 1/2 on Q2. Fixed: at = idx + 1.
  3. The greeting on the first turn ("Hi there! Which is bigger...") broke the
     same lookup by exact string match. Fixed: look for the question inside the
     turn.

  too lenient      34                 was 33

No regression and no big win: the corpus is dominated by the LUCKY GUESS

This fix was about a correct pupil being failed, and that is fixed.
